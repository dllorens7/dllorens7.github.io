Component 5: Project and Process Evaluation

Current Condition of Project And What We Think We Have Yet to Improve and/or Fix

By now we have reached the stage of the process where we have built a working product. We weren't able to succesfully put together a working battery made from scratch that would fulfill the power requirements of the Cyberdeck and work consistently in time for the Innovation Schowcase at Belen, so we ended up using a portable battery that we found and purchased online. That is the battery that is inside the battery as of now. That battery worked great, but for the purpose of trying to make as much of the project to actually be engineered and built by us and not just bought online, it would be great to actually succesfully design and construct our own battery from scratch. We might have to experiment with new different types of batteries and ways to build them. And obviously our final product battery will have certain constraints, such as being small enough to fit inside the Cyberdeck with the other pieces, not being too expensive, and, most importantly, achieving that long-lasting battery life (at least 24 hours) that is so essential to the mission and purpose of the Cyberdeck as a device you will be able to use for a long duration of time, useful for emergency scenarios.


Another unresolved technical problem we tried to make an improvised solution for a little before Belen's Innovation Showcase was limiting the maximum range of motion of the Cyberdeck's lid. This was a problem for two reasons. The first was a balance problem; if you opened up the top lid too much, then after a certain point the weight would be distributed too far back and the Cyberdeck would thus fall backwards and wouldn't stay upright unless you held it up, which is not convenient. The second reason was that the top lid was what had the Cyberdeck's screen, so if it retracted too far back, then the screen would be placed at a level where it wasn't perpendicular to your eyesight level. We tried to attack this lid range of motion problem by making some minor adjustments to the hinges, which are what allows for the rotation. We decided to superglue small wooden pieces to the hinges that would prevent the lid from retracting too far back, but ultimatly these wooden peices broke off and were made useless after you opened and closed the lid a couple of times. Part of the problem with their pieces is the fact that they are made of wood, which makes them weak and easily malleable. The superglue is also pretty week and can be ripped off with enough force, which is what happened. We'll have to develop a more thought out fix that maybe uses its own shape and form to attach to the hinge, rather than being stuck to it by some type of adhesive.


A last issue we have to deal with is being able to efficiently organize and fit all the different hardware components, parts, and cables inside the cyberdeck. In our latest design we would have to fit the keyboard inside the Cyberdeck in a specific, particular way for it to close well. And even then, the Cyberdeck was left open a tiny crack. We have to redisign the interior so that it all fits well together and there is no problem closing it. This is also important because doing so will allow us to save space inside the cyberdeck that will be room for more components and parts or to make the case/design smaller.
